You are a senior full-stack engineer and solution architect. Generate a complete, working local POC application for the following hackathon idea.

Project Name:
AI-Powered Clinical Trial Patient Pre-Screening Assistant

Business Context:
Clinical Research Organizations receive clinical trial protocols from pharmaceutical sponsors. These protocols contain complex inclusion and exclusion criteria. Clinical experts manually interpret the protocol and convert criteria into screening questions for recruiters. Recruiters then call potential patients and ask many questions to determine likely eligibility.

The POC should demonstrate how AI can help recruiters conduct patient pre-screening more efficiently by:
1. Uploading a sample clinical trial protocol PDF.
2. Extracting inclusion and exclusion criteria.
3. Generating simple, patient-friendly screening questions.
4. Grouping questions into two sections:
   - Demographic Information
   - Eligibility Criteria Questions
5. Starting a recruiter-led screening session.
6. Asking adaptive questions one at a time based on previous answers.
7. Sequencing eligibility criteria questions intelligently to support faster screening.
8. Explaining why each question is being asked and which protocol criterion or criteria it maps to.
9. Generating a final recommendation:
   - Likely Eligible
   - Likely Ineligible
   - Needs Clinical Review
10. Providing supporting reasons, missing information, and next action.

Important Positioning:
This is a hackathon POC, not a production clinical decisioning system. The system must not make final medical, diagnostic, or recruitment decisions. It should clearly position the output as pre-screening guidance for recruiter and clinical reviewer review.

The application must include the following disclaimer in the UI:

“This tool provides AI-assisted pre-screening guidance only. Final eligibility must be confirmed by qualified clinical staff.”

Important Question Structuring Requirement:
The screening experience must have exactly two major question sections:

1. Demographic Information
2. Eligibility Criteria Questions

Do not split the screening session into separate Inclusion Criteria and Exclusion Criteria sections.

Reason:
Separating all inclusion questions first and all exclusion questions later can waste recruiter time. For example, if the first exclusion question disqualifies the patient, it is inefficient to ask all inclusion questions before reaching it.

The system must support faster screening by intelligently sequencing eligibility criteria questions across both inclusion and exclusion criteria.

D. Question Bank Generation
For each approved criterion, generate patient-friendly screening questions.

The question bank must be generated into exactly two sections:

1. Demographic Information
2. Eligibility Criteria Questions

Demographic Information section:
- Must be generated first.
- Includes basic patient/profile/logistical questions that are useful across the screening workflow.
- Examples:
  - Age or age range
  - Sex at birth only if explicitly required by the protocol
  - Pregnancy status only if relevant to the protocol
  - Ability to provide consent
  - Ability to attend required visits
  - Location or travel ability if relevant
  - General patient profile questions required to assess eligibility

Eligibility Criteria Questions section:
- Contains all remaining inclusion and exclusion criteria questions.
- Inclusion and exclusion questions must not be separated into different UI sections.
- The sequence must be optimized for faster screening.
- High-impact exclusion criteria should generally be asked early if they can quickly disqualify a patient.
- High-priority inclusion criteria may also be asked early if failure would make the patient unlikely to qualify.
- Questions should be ordered by clinical/business screening value, not simply by inclusion first and exclusion second.

Question sequencing requirements:
- Ask demographic questions first.
- Then ask eligibility criteria questions in an AI-recommended optimized order.
- The optimized order should consider:
  - High-priority exclusion criteria that can quickly disqualify the patient
  - High-priority inclusion criteria that are essential for eligibility
  - Criteria that are easy for the patient to answer
  - Criteria that avoid unnecessary follow-up questions
  - Criteria that are likely to stop the screening early
  - Criteria that require clinical review if uncertain
- The system must not blindly ask all inclusion criteria before exclusion criteria.
- The system must not blindly ask all exclusion criteria before inclusion criteria either.
- The AI or backend should produce a recommended mixed sequence for the Eligibility Criteria Questions section.

De-duplication requirements:
- If a demographic question already covers an inclusion or exclusion criterion, that criterion must not be asked again separately in the Eligibility Criteria Questions section.
- Instead, the demographic question must store linked criteria.
- A single demographic question may cover one or multiple criteria.
- The answer to a demographic question must update the status of all linked criteria.
- The final summary must still show which inclusion or exclusion criteria were covered by demographic answers.
- The app must not ask the same information twice unless a follow-up is clinically necessary.

F. Adaptive Next-Best Question Logic
Implement controlled adaptive logic. Do not let the LLM randomly control the flow.

Recommended logic:
1. Ask all required demographic questions first.
2. Use demographic answers to satisfy, fail, or flag linked inclusion/exclusion criteria.
3. Do not ask criteria questions already covered by demographic answers.
4. After demographics, ask eligibility criteria questions in optimized mixed order.
5. The optimized order should mix inclusion and exclusion criteria based on screening value.
6. High-priority exclusion criteria that can quickly disqualify the patient should generally be asked early.
7. High-priority inclusion criteria that are essential for likely eligibility should also be asked early.
8. If an answer clearly fails an exclusion criterion, mark likely ineligible but allow recruiter to continue or end session.
9. If an answer clearly fails a must-have inclusion criterion, mark likely ineligible or needs review depending on criterion type.
10. If answer is unclear, ask a follow-up question or mark Needs Clinical Review.
11. Skip questions that are no longer relevant based on previous answers.
12. If required information is missing, mark as Needs Clinical Review.
13. Keep deterministic screening state in backend.

The backend must maintain deterministic screening state:
- Criteria satisfied
- Criteria failed
- Criteria needing review
- Criteria unanswered
- Criteria covered by demographic questions
- Criteria skipped due to de-duplication
- Criteria skipped due to adaptive logic
- Current section
- Section progress
- Overall likely status
